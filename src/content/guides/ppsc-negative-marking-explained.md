## Does PPSC have negative marking?
Some PPSC syllabi state it clearly. For example, the BS-19 Director posts we reviewed say that **0.25 marks are deducted for each wrong MCQ answer**. Many General Ability syllabi do not mention it, so **read the rules in your own advertisement and roll number slip**. On this site, mock exams use 0.25 negative marking so you practise the harder case.

## How the score is calculated
Net score = correct answers − (0.25 × wrong answers). Unanswered questions score zero.

Example: 100 questions, 70 correct, 20 wrong, 10 skipped. Net score = 70 − 5 = **65**.

## Should you guess?
With four options, a completely random guess has a one in four chance of being right.

- Right: +1 × 0.25 probability = +0.25
- Wrong: −0.25 × 0.75 probability = −0.1875
- Expected gain: **+0.0625 marks**

So even a blind guess slightly helps on average, but the variance is large. A guess gets much better after you eliminate options:

| Options eliminated | Chance of being right | Expected marks |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25% | +0.06 |
| 1 | 33% | +0.17 |
| 2 | 50% | +0.38 |

## A simple rule
- **Answer** when you can eliminate at least one option.
- **Skip** when you have no idea and the question needs a specific fact (a date, a name, an amount).
- Never leave easy marks: Urdu, English and computer questions are usually worth answering.

## If there is no negative marking
Answer everything. A blank is always worth zero.

## Practise it
Take a [mock exam](/exam/) and compare your raw score (correct answers) with your net score. If the gap is large, you are guessing too much.
