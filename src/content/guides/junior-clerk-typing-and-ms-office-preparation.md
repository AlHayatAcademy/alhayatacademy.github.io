The [Junior Clerk syllabus](/posts/junior-clerk/) has two parts: **about 80% General Ability** and **about 20% MS Office and computer skills**. Candidates who qualify the written General Ability test are then called for the English typing and proficiency test (in several advertisements, at a ratio of 1:20).

## MS Office questions in the written paper
Roughly one in five questions. They are recall questions you can learn in days:

- **Word:** shortcuts (Ctrl+B, I, U, E, L, R, J, F, H), header and footer, mail merge, page orientation.
- **Excel:** formulas start with =; functions SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN, COUNT, IF, VLOOKUP; cell references and absolute references with $.
- **PowerPoint:** slide show shortcuts, slide master, extensions (.pptx, .ppsx).
- **Windows and files:** Recycle Bin, shortcuts such as Ctrl+C, V, X, Z, Alt+Tab, file extensions.
- **Basics:** RAM vs ROM, memory units, input and output devices, internet terms.

Practise with our [Computer & IT tests](/subjects/computer-it/), ideally while using a real computer.

## The typing test
The Junior Clerk syllabus says English typing, with a separate proficiency test for those who pass. For the typing test:

1. Practise daily for 20 to 30 minutes using a free typing tutor.
2. Learn touch typing (home row first) so you do not look at the keys.
3. Aim for accuracy first, then speed.
4. Practise typing from printed text, not just on-screen.
5. Know the basics of formatting in Word: bold, alignment, save as.

Check your advertisement for the speed required.

## Plan
Allocate 2 days to MS Office MCQs during your General Ability preparation, and keep 20 minutes of typing every day from the start.
