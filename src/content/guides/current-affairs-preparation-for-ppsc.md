Current Affairs is a listed subject in nearly every PPSC General Ability syllabus, but static facts (history, geography, science) usually carry as many marks. A focused plan beats reading every news story.

## How far back should you go?
Cover **the last 6 to 12 months** before your test date. Older events become general knowledge and are better studied from history or Pakistan Studies notes.

## Topics that repeat
- **Appointments and office-holders:** President, Prime Minister, Chief Justice, Chief Minister, Governors, army and service chiefs.
- **Elections and constitutional amendments:** dates, numbers, what changed.
- **Economy:** budget figures, major projects, central bank decisions, trade partners.
- **International organisations:** summits, new members, headquarters, secretaries-general.
- **Awards and sports:** Nobel Prizes, major cups, Pakistani achievements.
- **Days and observances:** the UN and Pakistan calendar.

## A daily routine (20 minutes)
1. Read one national newspaper's main headlines and the Pakistan page.
2. Write every name, number and date you find into a notebook, one line each.
3. At the weekend turn the notes into your own 10 MCQs.

## Avoid out-of-date answers
Office-holders change. In the **last week** before the test, re-check: who holds each major office, recent amendments and recent international events. Facts in our current affairs tests carry a note that they must be verified.
